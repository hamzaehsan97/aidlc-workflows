# Product discovery and validation

Use the existing Ideation stages; do not introduce a second approval engine.
A small feature may reuse recent evidence. An incident fix need not repeat market
research. A new product needs a recorded investment decision before major buildout.
If the selected scope skips Ideation (for example an MVP path), attach the discovery
brief as input or explicitly schedule Ideation; installing this plugin does not
force skipped stages to execute.

## Evidence before a build commitment

1. Define a narrow buyer, user, costly recurring job and current workaround.
   Distinguish buyer from user and company tenant from individual identity.
2. List the riskiest assumptions: problem frequency, willingness to pay, access
   to buyers, acquisition cost, integration effort, data access and support burden.
3. Choose the cheapest test for each: interviews, a prototype, concierge delivery,
   a landing-page experiment or a paid pilot. Set the observation window, spend
   cap, success threshold and stop condition before seeing results.
4. Record observed evidence with date, source and permission to use it. Separate
   direct quotes, observed behavior, inference and unknowns. Keep raw sensitive
   interviews privately; this public extension contains no customer evidence.
5. Compare results with the precommitted thresholds. Recommend proceed, pivot,
   defer or stop, with confidence and disconfirming evidence. The product owner
   decides. A timeboxed prototype can proceed with unknowns if its experiment
   budget and boundaries are explicit; that does not authorize a production launch.

Example starting experiment, to adapt rather than treat as proof: interview five
qualified buyers, observe three independently describing the same costly job,
and seek two concrete pilot commitments. A signup or positive interview is weaker
than recurring use or payment. Small samples are directional, not market-size proof.

## Handoff and learning

The initiative brief includes target segment, evidence links, chosen experiment,
results, price hypothesis, estimated delivery/support cost, go/no-go decision,
owner, date, next review and smallest viable customer outcome. Reuse the engine's
initiative-brief and decision-log artifacts; do not create shadow state files.

Slice the first build around one measurable customer journey. Each work item has
an outcome, acceptance criteria, telemetry, dependencies and platform contracts.
After a pilot, compare activation, repeated use, conversion, support effort and
cost per tenant with the brief. Update the decision instead of letting an
unvalidated prototype grow into the portfolio by default.
